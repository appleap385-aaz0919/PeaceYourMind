# Daily life-tips Short workflow

## Default demonstration style

For every new manual sample and scheduled life-tip release, follow the accepted T-shirt-bag sample: original AI-photorealistic hand-action photos, consistent objects, closeups of the critical manipulation, intermediate states, and a visible finished result. Show the payoff first. Use short per-scene `heading` text and readable captions around a large unobscured photo; the renderer adds `AI 재현`. Do not represent synthetic scenes as a physically filmed test. Keep full-diagram/cartoon sequences out of the default format. This applies only to `youtube_life_tips`; keep PIM unchanged.

Unattended production uses no browser or persistent preview server. If a manual task explicitly starts a preview, record its exact session/PID and project command line, then stop only that owned server after render/review/export on success or failure and verify it exited. Never kill by port alone or stop another project's server.

This workflow is launched by the project's Windows Task Scheduler mailbox. It is not a Codex Scheduled task. The watcher invokes separate, short-lived Codex CLI sessions for the main writer, reviewer, visual developer, and final auditor; they exchange JSON and image files under `work/mailbox/` and `work/daily/`.

Enqueue the morning request at 03:00 Asia/Seoul for 07:00 release, and a separate evening request at 15:00 for 19:00 release. The upload cutoffs are 06:30 and 18:30. Each release has at most three attempts with 1-minute then 3-minute waits; successful stages and validated renders are reused. Missed starts and validation failures skip only that release without catch-up. An uncertain ImageGen attempt must never be repeated for the same release. Morning keys keep the legacy `YYYY-MM-DD` name; evening keys are `YYYY-MM-DD-pm`. Use the release key for daily folders, runs, requests, and reports.

Work only inside `youtube_life_tips`. Do not read or use the parent Peace in Mind project, its credentials, assets, or channel. Do not use Gemini, paid APIs, downloaded media, or prior/sample visuals. Use the built-in `$imagegen` exactly once per release for one new storyboard with a panel per scene. If ImageGen is unavailable, fails, reaches a limit, or cannot save into the release folder, skip that release.

## Research selection rule

Read `EDITORIAL_BRIEF.md` before discovery and topic approval. Apply the user's recorded rejections and result-based selection criteria to automatic and manual drafts.

Use the current Korean season for topic selection: spring is March-May, summer June-August, autumn September-November, and winter December-February. Discover real problems broadly through creator videos, practitioner writing, consumer reporting, forums, Q&A, and user reviews; do not exclude a candidate because its discovery source is non-institutional. Separate discovery from verification: support factual claims with at least two independent sources, allowing credible practitioner/creator sources for low-risk tips when context is clear and cross-checked. Use an appropriate primary or technical source for machine-specific, chemical, health, or safety claims; label subjective experience and never present an anecdote as universal fact. Identify each source's supported claim and do not count syndicated copies as independent. If no seasonal tip is both well-supported and genuinely useful, use a season-neutral household tip and explain the fallback in `fallback_reason`. Never force a seasonal angle or invent seasonal evidence. Record `topic_strategy` as `seasonal` or `evergreen_fallback`; the independent reviewer must verify the season fit or fallback reason.

Compare at least three useful candidates from different household categories before choosing. Rank them by how often viewers face the problem, the time, effort, or waste saved, whether the payoff is visible, and whether the complete result and its necessary choices have source support. When the last three uploaded tips are food, prefer an equally useful, sourced non-food candidate. This is a preference, not a quota: a better-supported food tip may still win. Record the comparison in `category_choice_reason`, and have the reviewer check `category_balance_pass`.

Before selecting a topic, inspect at least five already-published 생활꿀팁 posts/videos from at least three independent creators. Search creator channels, short-form platforms, forums, and practical blogs; do not search only institutional reference pages. For each candidate reference, note its audience/situation, the specific action shown, the visible before/after or measurable payoff, the unexpected element, and any safety/factual claim that needs checking. Use audience response only to discover what attracts attention; views, likes, comments, or a creator's confidence do not verify the tip. Do not copy a reference's script, shot sequence, distinctive expression, or visuals. Extract the underlying editorial pattern, then independently verify the proposed method. Reject references whose promise is clickbait, unsafe, unsupported, or merely a familiar chore.

Evidence must support the editorial score. A 4+ problem-relevance score can use either path, linked or named in `category_choice_reason`: (A) a recognized problem, with at least two independent viewer-side signals from different people/sources; or (B) an underknown benefit, with at least two independent signals validating that the everyday situation is common, plus credible evidence for a consequential hidden effect or missed benefit. Path B must name the affected viewer, observable situation, newly useful action, and material payoff. Do not invent prevalence or claim people complain about an issue they may not notice. Creator comments, forums, product Q&A/reviews, consumer reporting, and practitioner writing can help establish discovery or common context, but do not substantiate a fix; verify factual claims independently. Do not infer demand from views alone. A topic whose main conclusion is simply "read the product label/manual and follow it" fails novelty and usefulness even when accurate. Require an independently verified, non-obvious finding that changes a viewer decision and yields a concrete payoff; otherwise reject the topic.

## Content-level repetition check

Before drafting, compare the idea with `work/sample/metadata.json`, `work/previews/*/content.json`, and previous `work/daily/*/content.json` records, including the other release on the same date. Exclude only the current release's own content from comparison. Avoid repeating the same actionable recommendation, central factual claim, solution steps, or distinctive hook. Repeating a broad subject, appliance, room, or category is acceptable when the practical advice and editorial angle are meaningfully different.

## Viewer value and narration flow

Record `viewer_value.common_approach`, `better_decision`, and `why_it_matters`. The better decision must be a sourced, non-obvious condition, threshold, exception, or technique that materially changes what the viewer does. A hidden-benefit topic must show the ordinary situation, the fact most viewers may not know, what action that fact changes, and the meaningful time, money, effort, waste, comfort, or safety payoff. Being little-known is not enough: reject trivia that changes no decision or produces no material benefit. Reject scripts whose takeaway is merely "put fruit in the refrigerator" or "fold clothes into a drawer," even if those statements are correct. A fact that can be removed without changing the obvious advice is decorative, not viewer value. Repeating a broad household category is allowed when the actual advice is useful and distinct. Read all scene narrations aloud as one script: each scene must advance the explanation, transitions must make sense, and the ending must add a payoff rather than repeat the advice. The reviewer must explain and pass both `usefulness_pass` and `flow_pass` before image generation. The final audit checks both again.

Require a demonstrable payoff for practical-tip formats: the viewer should be able to see a transformation, compare a result, diagnose a real condition, complete a repair/shortcut, or avoid a specific cost/failure. If the method cannot be demonstrated, state exactly what credible evidence and viewer decision make the narration worth watching; generic explanation or maintenance alone does not qualify. Prefer a small number of complete, useful tips over a list padded to meet runtime. A popular hack is a discovery lead, not proof that it works or is safe.

## Natural spoken Korean

For every new or revised `spoken_script`, read the repository-local im-not-ai skill at `../.agents/skills/humanize-korean/SKILL.md` and its bundled quick rules. Establish the takeaway and explanation order in a source-checked draft first, apply the skill's single-call style pass before scene splitting, then recheck meaning and sentence connections. This skill directory is the permitted parent-folder writing reference; generated output stays inside `youtube_life_tips`. Keep style diagnostics outside narration, captions, and automated JSON responses.

Write narration for the ear, not as an article. Read the full script aloud at normal pace before splitting it into scenes; revise translated or bureaucratic wording, stiff written endings, unexplained English abbreviations, multi-idea sentences, and joins that sound like separate captions stitched together. Prefer everyday words, short sentences with one main thought, and spoken transitions that make cause, condition, and next action clear. Translate institutional acronyms into a natural Korean name on first mention. The independent reviewer must pass `naturalness_pass` and quote any awkward phrase for repair; the final rendered audit must pass `spoken_korean_valid` against the actual narration.

Record `viewer_value.new_insight` as the one-sentence new decision a viewer learns. The independent reviewer must pass `novelty_pass` separately from usefulness and explain `novelty_reason`. Reject an idea whose main action is routine after removing numbers, jargon, or decorative facts. The final audit must pass `novelty_valid` on the rendered narration before upload.

Use the same result-first editorial standard for manual samples and scheduled videos. Keep 65-90 seconds for shared YouTube/TikTok exports. The selected demonstration must earn this length through useful teaching, not filler or artificially slow speech. A complete transformation, repair, or shortcut may have one sourced `viewer_value.decision_points` entry (`when`, `do`, `result`). Two or three useful applications of one household item each need their own entry and material payoff. Do not invent diagnostic branches or turn prerequisites into separate decisions. Apply the 20-second compression test to remove unnecessary explanation, not to impose a quota of diagnostic branches. A technique too short for 65 seconds should be reported as a short candidate rather than padded. The reviewer must pass `density_pass` with a `density_reason` explaining the concrete result, reproducibility, and absence of padding. The final audit must pass `density_valid` against the actual narration and frames. Keep a clear shared problem, result, or reused object throughout the video.

## Daily handoff

1. Main researches the seasonal-first topic and writes `draft.json` with a continuous spoken_script split into as many original Korean narration/caption scenes as needed, viewer value, and at least two HTTPS sources.
2. Reviewer independently verifies the cited claims, safety, usefulness, narration flow, content-level originality, and season selection. Editorial issues receive up to two revisions with independent re-review before ImageGen; unresolved factual or safety issues mean skip.
3. Main incorporates review findings and writes `approved.json`. The plan must record the date, season, `topic_strategy`, fallback reason when relevant, title, description, scenes, and sources. Keep source titles and URLs in internal source records only; the public YouTube description is a concise viewer summary without references, citations, or source URLs.
4. Developer makes exactly one new ImageGen storyboard and saves `work/daily/RELEASE-KEY/storyboard.png`. Reused or duplicate art is rejected.
5. The local renderer creates and checks a 720x1280, 15fps video with Korean Edge TTS and one FFmpeg thread.
6. Main audits the generated storyboard, final content, metadata, duration, dimensions, audio, sources, and season decision. Only a complete approval permits upload. For automatic and manually rendered videos, complete the TikTok handoff in the project README using `codex_mailbox.save_tiktok_copy(video_path, day, slot)` and confirm the dated MP4 exists before reporting completion.
7. `upload_daily.py --date YYYY-MM-DD --slot am|pm` verifies the channel and schedules the private upload for 07:00 or 19:00. The renderer accepts the same slot argument.

Write the final status to `work/mailbox/outbox/RELEASE-KEY.json` and logs to `work/mailbox/runs/RELEASE-KEY/`. The local runner sends the release result email when secure email notifications are configured. Never commit credentials, tokens, videos, or daily work products.

## Comprehension and layout repair

Write spoken_script before splitting scenes; the ordered scene narrations must reproduce it without losing transitions. Use the number of scenes the explanation needs, never force four. Every scene needs visual_action matching its object, state, and instruction. Write all expressive elements independently: do not copy or closely imitate source scripts, hooks, titles, distinctive scene order, graphics, footage, photos, or audio. New narration and visuals do not excuse copying distinctive expressive choices; a source link is attribution, not permission. YouTube's reused-content and inauthentic-content monetization reviews are separate from copyright; each video must add substantial original educational value, not merely paraphrase a source or slightly vary a mass-produced template. The reviewer must return viewer_recap, scene_count, and transition_checks for every adjacent pair (from_scene, to_scene, clear, evidence quoting actual wording). Do not infer missing steps from the description or sources. Check the complete final on-screen text, not merely legibility.

Before ImageGen, measure the text at the real rendering widths. Reflow and resize within readable limits, then revise long wording or split scenes if needed, up to two revisions with independent review. Never silently truncate words or omit conditions to fit. Generate the grid, narration clips, and audit contact sheet for the resulting scene count. New uploads set containsSyntheticMedia=true.


Transient preflight failures have a separate six-attempt budget with backoff capped at 30 minutes and bounded by the same release cutoff. They do not consume the three production attempts before any drafting begins. The Codex version probe waits at most 60 seconds; a successful probe is reused within the process only while the executable path, modification time, and size remain unchanged. Authentication failures and other non-transient errors still stop the run.


## Targeted visual repair

When an approved script fails final review because a panel misrepresents its action, preserve the script, voice and unaffected panels. Permit at most two built-in ImageGen edits of the current storyboard, one invocation per separately recorded attempt. An uncertain edit is never repeated. Re-render and independently audit the actual edited video after each edit. Lower density or viewer-pull scores caused by the same picture defect may be repaired this way; facts, sources, originality, narration and all sixteen final checks still have to pass. Do not discard a useful approved topic before these bounded repairs. Preserve the original image and review under `repairs/`.
